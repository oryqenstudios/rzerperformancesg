# Real Evo IX parts redraw RZER's configurator

HKS, Tomei and ENDLESS all make current parts for the Lancer Evolution IX (CT9A, 4G63 MIVEC), but only five kinds of part change what a photo of the car shows: the exhaust tip, the brakes, the ride height set by coilovers, the front-mount intercooler seen through the bumper, and a short list of engine-bay pieces (intake filters, HKS's carbon plug cover and coil packs, blow-off valves, the turbo, Tomei's heat shield and oil caps). Everything else these brands sell for the car, from camshafts and stroker kits to fuel pumps, ECUs, clutches and brake pads, belongs on the job card as a text line. The biggest correction to today's configurator is the exhaust: **every HKS and Tomei Evo IX cat-back with an official photo has one round tip, 106–124 mm across, in the factory position under the right of the rear bumper**, so the site's twin, quad and centre layouts have no real counterpart and should give way to brand-named single tips in polished, heat-blued or dark titanium finishes ([HKS muffler table](https://www.hks-power.co.jp/en/product_search/product/1/maker/5); [Tomei Expreme Ti](https://www.tomei-p.co.jp/catalogue/ti-evo7-9/)). Brakes are the richest visible category: ENDLESS lists **30 CT9A caliper kits**, standard in blue anodise with custom colours that map straight onto the existing caliper-colour picker, and HKS's own Evo brake kits carry ENDLESS kit codes ([Endless USA kit search](https://endlessusa.com/product-search/bsk/by-vehicle); [HKS Brake System](https://www.hks-power.co.jp/en/product_search/product/551/maker/5)). Availability is uneven: most HKS lines are current, while Tomei's signature Expreme Ti cat-back is being run out ahead of a renewed model. In Singapore, HKS has an exclusive distributor, no official Tomei channel was found, and RZER's own shop site already sells ENDLESS kits. Exhaust and turbo changes need LTA approval before fitting, catalytic converter removal is banned, and since 27 February 2026 workshops face fines of up to **S$40,000** ([The Independent SG](https://theindependent.sg/lta-warns-against-illegal-vehicle-mods-as-workshop-fined-and-penalties-raised/)). None of the three brands publishes a policy on dealers using its product photos, so every cut-out needs written permission.

## Five kinds of part change the photo; the rest are job-card lines

The configurator shows a generated stock Evo IX from eight angles around the car (`a000`, the front, through `a315`, turning towards the car's right side), close-ups of the wheel, exhaust, headlight and roof, four cabin views and two engine-bay views, and lays each option over the photo as its own layer. Judged against those views, the three catalogues sort cleanly. A part earns a photo layer only if a visitor would see it: the exhaust tip from behind, the calipers and discs through the spokes, the change in stance, the intercooler core in the bumper opening, and the items that sit on top of the engine. Tomei's database lists no body or aero parts for the Evo, and the HKS and ENDLESS research turned up no aero, wheels or lights for the car either, so the configurator's paint, wheel finish, aero, lights and tint options stay unbranded ([Tomei 4G63 database](https://www.tomei-p.co.jp/products/?type=3&keyword=&engineOnly=4G63)).

## Every real Evo IX exhaust leaves through one round tip

HKS Japan lists exactly **three JDM cat-backs for the Evo IX and IX MR**: the Super Turbo Muffler 31029-AM001, the silent Hi-Power 31019-AM008 and the titanium Hi-Power SPEC-R 31030-AM001. HKS makes no Legamax, Hi-Power SPEC-L, Super Dragger or Cool Style for any CT9A; the Legamax Premium 31021-AM004/AM006, SPEC-L 31019-AM011 and Super Turbo Muffler 31029-AM004 that turn up in searches are Evo X (CZ4A) parts and must not be listed ([HKS muffler table](https://www.hks-power.co.jp/en/product_search/product/1/maker/5)). Every official on-car Evo IX photo shows one tip in the stock place under the right of the rear bumper ([Super Turbo on car](https://www.hks-power.co.jp/storage/syshks/product_db/8YCcTIkhJdachkto1TlAyMShMrqGLHNw42ci8Wem.jpeg); [silent Hi-Power on car](https://www.hks-power.co.jp/storage/syshks/product_db/Lz0txJPPxlWnKnhkrcqRjijf7ZDT7CPS4fXGEVki.jpeg); [SPEC-R on car](https://www.hks-power.co.jp/storage/syshks/product_db/H0ufy57qrzOH8YC110jGN03v1NQd42fMcfLTJKiv.jpeg)). ### LTA gates exhausts and turbos, and fines now reach S$40,000

LTA sorts modifications into those needing no approval, those needing approval and those not allowed, and requires all of them to be fitted by qualified people following the vehicle and product makers' instructions ([OneMotoring modifications](https://onemotoring.lta.gov.sg/content/onemotoring/home/owning/modification.html), read from search extracts because the site was blocked). The exhaust rule is the firmest: approval comes **before** fitting, on the strength of the maker's certificate that the system suits the make and model plus noise and emission test reports from an LTA- or NEA-recognised laboratory, and "You are not allowed to remove catalytic converters and mufflers" (same source). LTA's 2021 enforcement release adds that only certified systems tested to international standards and compatible with the model are allowed ([LTA newsroom 2021](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/newsroom/2021/5/news-releases/workshop-owner-charged-for-illegal-exhaust-modifications.html)). **No published LTA list of approved car exhausts was found**, so no HKS or Tomei system can be called "LTA-approved" without the paperwork for that car. This is where the brands part ways: HKS's JDM mufflers carry Japanese certificate numbers and published noise figures, while Tomei's titanium system is "for off-road use only" with no dB figure, which leaves little to submit; even HKS's Japanese certificates do not carry over automatically, since LTA wants reports for the model from a lab it or NEA recognises. Penalties rose sharply **from 27 February 2026** to S$20,000 and up to two years' jail for individuals and **S$40,000 for workshops**, doubled for repeat offenders; **26 workshops** have been charged since 2021, and one was fined S$6,000 in 2026 for five illegal exhaust jobs ([The Independent SG](https://theindependent.sg/lta-warns-against-illegal-vehicle-mods-as-workshop-fined-and-penalties-raised/); [Mothership](https://mothership.sg/2026/04/workshop-fined-illegal-modification-exhaust-singapore/)). Since April 2018 NEA has also limited hydrocarbons, not just carbon monoxide, for in-use petrol cars, so a removed cat or rich tune risks failing periodic inspection as well ([NEA](https://www.nea.gov.sg/media/news/news/reducing-pollution-from-in-use-vehicles-to-achieve-better-air-quality)).

### No brand publishes photo rules, so every cut-out needs permission

None of HKS, Tomei or ENDLESS publishes a policy on dealers using its logos or product photos, and free logo downloads on logo sites grant no licence. The norm is written permission and official media from the distributor: Garage R for HKS, Tomei Japan directly (it keeps a logo page for dealers and press), and ENDLESS through its Singapore distributor contact ([HKS Europe trade information](https://www.hkseurope.com/?p=13); [Tomei logoset](https://www.tomei-p.co.jp/logoset/); [ENDLESS Singapore](https://endless-sport.global/sg)). Copying images from retailers such as Blackhawk Japan is riskier still, because those images belong to the maker or the retailer. Brand and model names used plainly to say what a part is carry less risk than logos and photos. ## Conclusion

The research turns the configurator from a styling toy into something closer to a catalogue, and the parts that make that change are the ones with the best paperwork and photos. Brake calipers are the strongest hero category: ENDLESS has a deep, current CT9A range, white-background official shots, a clear colour rulebook that already matches the site's four caliper colours, and the client almost certainly sells it today. That makes the brake page a natural storefront for a business RZER already runs. The exhaust is the most important correction: switching to single, brand-named tips makes the site truthful at the very point an enthusiast checks first. HKS's certified JDM mufflers are the only plausible candidates for LTA approval, while Tomei's titanium system is being run out and is off-road only.

The deeper implication is that the most desirable brand parts (turbo kits, strokers, titanium exhausts, decats) are also the ones that are invisible or legally fraught, so the job card does more work than the photo. Each job-card line should carry three facts the research supplies: the maker's current, back-order or discontinued status, any combination rule (an HKS intercooler with an HKS intake, no SSQV4; Tomei springs with Evo 7 or titanium retainers; no HKS cam pulley on an IX, and HKS's intake cam only once a dealer confirms it suits MIVEC), and the LTA label. Three conversations unlock the rest: an image pack and test documents from Garage R, photo permission from Tomei Japan, and the client's confirmation of its ENDLESS dealer status. Until those land, plain brand and model names over the site's own generated layers are the safe way to launch.
