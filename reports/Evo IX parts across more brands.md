# Wheels, brakes and aero redraw RZER's Evo IX

Almost every brand on RZER's wider list still sells current parts for the Lancer Evolution IX (CT9A, 4G63 MIVEC), but the ones that change the configurator's photos are concentrated in five places: **wheels and tyres, brake discs and big brake kits, coilover stance, bolt-on aero, and a short list of engine-bay pieces** (intakes, intercoolers, radiators, strut bars, coloured hoses and mounts), plus the **exhaust tip** and a handful of cabin parts (seats, steering wheels, gauges, cages, a short shifter). The rest, from forged pistons and camshafts to clutches, rear differentials, fuel pumps and brake pads, belongs on the job card. The wheel market is the deepest: Rays, Enkei, BBS, WedsSport, SSR and Advan all make current 5x114.3 wheels at the classic aggressive Evo size of **18x9.5 +22 to +45**, and Rays is the only maker that tags specific sizes as clearing the Evo VII–IX Brembo calipers ([Rays TE37 SAGA SL](https://www.rayswheels.co.jp/products/brand/detail/303)). Three findings correct assumptions in the brief: **every Evo IX cat-back found from Blitz, Fujitsubo, Kakimoto, Invidia and ETS has one round tip at the factory right-rear exit**, so no real product needs the site's twin, quad or centre layouts ([Kakimoto Evo IX list](https://www.kakimotoracing.co.jp/products/list_cartype.cgi?rid=877)); **ETS, AMS and Buschur no longer list an Evo 8/9 downpipe**, and AMS no longer lists its Evo 8/9 intake or intercooler; and **Garrett makes no Evo 8/9 turbo kit of its own**, its upgrade kits being for the Evo X ([Garrett instructions](https://www.garrettmotion.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/Evo_Upgrade_Kit_Installation_Instructions_RevA.pdf)). Several famous names are run-out or gone: the Work Emotion CR Kiwami (succeeded by the CR Shigoku in December 2025), the Bridgestone RE-71RS (replaced by the RE-71RZ from February 2026), both Blitz Nür-Spec Evo IX exhausts, Varis's carbon lip, canard and fender variants (February 2026) and the shift-knob maker Raceseng, which has closed. In Singapore, official distributors exist for Rays and WedsSport (Stamford Tyres), Work (Leong Seng), BBS (South East Tyre, CCY Autosport), Enkei, OZ and Yokohama tyres (YHI), Project Mu, AP Racing, Alcon, BC Racing and Varis; almost everything else arrives through SGD-priced importers such as **RZ Crew Garage** and **Blackhawk Japan SG**.

## Twenty-odd visible categories map onto the configurator's existing views

The photo configurator shows a stock Evo IX from eight angles (`a000` head-on through `a315`), close-ups of the wheel, exhaust, headlight and roof, two engine-bay views and four cabin views (dashboard from the rear seat, front seats through the driver's door, the rear bench, and the steering wheel and gauges). The research sorted each part by where a visitor would actually see it. Wheels, tyres, discs, calipers and ride height show in every exterior view and in the wheel close-up; front aero and intercooler cores show in the front views (`a000`, `a045`, `a315`); exhaust tips, diffusers and wings show in the rear views and the exhaust close-up; intakes, intercooler piping, radiators, strut bars, coloured hoses, top mounts and engine mounts show in the two engine-bay views; and seats, wheels, gauges, cages, harnesses and the short shifter show in the cabin views.

Three conventions apply throughout. Prices are **reference only for internal quoting**, in the currency the source printed: Japanese yen are makers' list prices (Rays, Enkei, WedsSport, BBS, Advan and Tein include 10% tax; Varis and Do-Luck figures are ex-tax), US dollars are maker or US retailer prices, and SGD figures come from RZ Crew Garage (which states import taxes are included) or Blackhawk Japan SG. "Current" means the maker or a specialist retailer listed the item as orderable on 26 November 2026, not that it is in stock. And because the client's brief puts **no text on the car**, the look descriptions note where a real part carries logos (Mishimoto's "M" on its intercooler core, OZ's printed rim lettering, caliper logos) so generated layers can leave them out or the client can sign off on them.

## Wheels are the richest visual choice, with eighteen-inch sizes from every maker

Two OZ options deserve care rather than a slot. The **RR40 and Rally Racing designs depend on large printed "O.Z RACING" lettering** round the face, which the no-text rule would erase, and the magnesium **Superturismo Magnesio** (18x9 +32, 8.43 kg) is a limited edition that OZ sells for track use ([OZ RR40](https://www.ozracing.com/jp/wheels/oz-racing/sport/RR40); [OZ Superturismo Magnesio](https://www.ozracing.com/alloy-wheels/oz-racing/Mag-Limited/superturismo-magnesio)). ### Tyres show only as sidewall and stance, and the RE-71RS is being replaced

All four requested tyres come in the Evo's sizes, from the stock **235/45R17** to the aggressive **265/35R18**, and all carry raised black lettering with no coloured logos, so a plain black sidewall satisfies the brief. The **Yokohama Advan Neova AD09** is the durable street-and-track tyre, with a visible rim-protector bar in every Evo size; the **Advan A052** is lighter (about 1 kg per tyre) with shallower 5.7 mm tread and an almost slick outer shoulder ([Yokohama AD09 sizes](https://www.y-yokohama.com/product/tire/advan_ad09_size/); [Yokohama A052 sizes](https://www.y-yokohama.com/product/tire/advan_a052_size/)). Bridgestone has **replaced the Potenza RE-71RS with the RE-71RZ, on sale in Japan from 1 February 2026**, claiming 1.2% faster dry and 1.1% faster wet laps; every RE-71RS Evo size is now marked limited quantity ([Bridgestone RE-71RZ](https://tire.bridgestone.co.jp/potenza/re71rz/); [RE-71RS sizes](https://tire.bridgestone.co.jp/potenza/re71rs/size.html)). Toyo markets the **Proxes R888R** as a DOT race tyre for competition events, a near-slick with a few deep grooves, so it should be presented as a track-day semi-slick ([Toyo R888R](https://www.toyotires.com/product/proxes-r888r/)). Matching sizes to wheels: 255/40R17 suits a 17x9, 245/40R18 an 18x8.5–9, 255/35R18 an 18x9–9.5 and 265/35R18 the aggressive 18x9.5. Japanese list prices run ¥75,570–85,800 per tyre for the RE-71RS at 18 inches and ¥77,770–91,080 for the RE-71RZ; Yokohama sells both Advans at open price.

## Conclusion

The wider brand list changes the configurator's job more than its parts list. The HKS, Tomei and ENDLESS catalogue mostly added things under the bonnet and behind the wheels; this set adds **whole new faces for the car**, namely wheel designs, bumpers, wings and bonnets, which the current photo system cannot show by recolouring. Each new wheel design or replacement bumper needs a fresh edited layer in every exterior view, so the client's choices should favour a small number of high-impact items: perhaps six wheel families, two or three bonnet styles, one pedestal wing per brand, and add-on aero that sits on the stock bumper. Coilover spring colour, disc hat colour and caliper colour, by contrast, are cheap to show and strongly brand-identifying, which makes them the best value per layer.

Supply is the hidden constraint. Many of the most photogenic parts (Varis carbon, Do-Luck kits, Voltex, Forced Performance turbos, Work wheels) are made to order with lead times of weeks to months, several famous models were dropped in 2025–2026, and the brands with official Singapore channels cluster in wheels, tyres, brakes and a few chassis makers. A practical next step is to tag each configurator option as "official Singapore channel", "stocked by an importer" or "import to order", and to confirm the open fitment questions (the Varis bonnet number, the Cusco 448 differential on the IX, the factory caliper colour, and 5-speed versus 6-speed parts) with the distributors before the client finalises the list.
