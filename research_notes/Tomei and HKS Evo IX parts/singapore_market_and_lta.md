HKS has a clear official channel: **Garage R Performance Centre** says it is the **exclusive HKS distributor for Singapore and Malaysia**, with a dealer network of 200+ workshops. **I found no official Tomei distributor for Singapore.** Tomei parts reach Singapore through Japan-sourcing online retailers that price in SGD (notably Blackhawk Japan, blackhawkjapan.sg) and import-tax-inclusive web shops (RZ Crew Garage), plus the second-hand market (Carousell).

## 2. What do LTA rules allow, require approval for, or prohibit, by part category (2025–2026)?

### Takeaway
LTA sorts modifications into three tiers: **no approval needed**, **approval needed**, and **not allowed**. **Exhaust changes and turbocharger/supercharger upgrades need prior LTA approval**, and removing catalytic converters or mufflers is not allowed. Engine swaps are only possible with approval, and increasing engine capacity and nitrous are reported as banned. **Penalties were raised sharply from 27 Feb 2026**: up to S$20,000 and/or 2 years' jail for individuals, up to S$40,000 for workshops, doubled for repeat offenders. LTA prosecutes workshops for illegal exhaust work. ECU remaps, intakes, BOVs and ground clearance aren't spelt out in any official source I could reach.

### Cited Findings
**Framework (official)**
- OneMotoring groups vehicle modifications into three types: those that don't need LTA approval, those that do, and those that aren't allowed. The banned group covers changes that compromise the safety of the vehicle's occupants and other road users, or pose a nuisance to the public. Modifications must follow LTA guidelines that protect road safety and the exhaust and noise standards. — [LTA OneMotoring, Vehicle Technologies and Modifications](https://onemotoring.lta.gov.sg/content/onemotoring/home/owning/modification.html) (search extract)
- All modifications must be installed by qualified personnel, following the vehicle and product manufacturers' recommendations. — [LTA OneMotoring](https://onemotoring.lta.gov.sg/content/onemotoring/home/owning/modification.html) (search extract)
- Examples on the official page: **no approval** covers bumpers, traction batteries and traction motors. **Not allowed** covers air horns, chassis changes and crash bars. The page links to a separate page on HID and LED headlamps, whose content I couldn't read. — [LTA OneMotoring](https://onemotoring.lta.gov.sg/content/onemotoring/home/owning/modification.html) (search extract)
- LTA issues Vehicle Approval Code (VAC) circulars to ESAs; VAC 34/2025 and VAC 35/2025 exist. I couldn't read their content. — [VAC 34 2025](https://onemotoring.lta.gov.sg/content/dam/onemotoring/pdf/Circulars%20to%20ESAs/2025/VAC_34_2025.pdf); [VAC 35 2025](https://onemotoring.lta.gov.sg/content/dam/onemotoring/pdf/Circulars%20to%20ESAs/2025/VAC_35_2025.pdf)

**Exhausts: approval required (official)**
- You must get LTA approval **before** replacing or changing the exhaust system. You submit the product maker's certification that the system suits your make and model, plus test reports from LTA- or NEA-recognised laboratories showing that it meets the noise and emission standards for your model. — [LTA OneMotoring](https://onemotoring.lta.gov.sg/content/onemotoring/home/owning/modification.html) (search extract)
- "You are not allowed to remove catalytic converters and mufflers, as it will adversely affect the exhaust emission characteristics of the vehicle." — [LTA OneMotoring](https://onemotoring.lta.gov.sg/content/onemotoring/home/owning/modification.html) (search extract)
- LTA, in a 2021 enforcement release: only certified exhaust systems "which have undergone stringent testing in compliance with international standards and are compatible with that particular make and model of the vehicle will be allowed". — [LTA newsroom, May 2021: workshop owner charged for illegal exhaust modifications](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/newsroom/2021/5/news-releases/workshop-owner-charged-for-illegal-exhaust-modifications.html)
- After LTA grants in-principle approval, the car is inspected at an LTA-authorised centre (STA Inspection, JIC or VICOM) through LTA's Vehicle Inspection & Type Approval System (VITAS). This comes from a forum post quoting LTA; it is secondhand and may be dated. — (search-result summary; the forum source URL was not given separately. Treat as unverified.)
- A commercial guide says LTA's static sound test is the official way exhaust noise is measured, but gives no dB figure for cars. — [Speedzone.sg, Exhaust laws explained](https://speedzone.sg/exhaust-laws-explained/)
- LTA's importation guidelines require noise test tracks to meet ISO 10844 (type-approval context). — [LTA, car importation guidelines (PDF)](https://onemotoring.lta.gov.sg/content/dam/onemotoring/Buying/PDF/Importation_Guidelines/Car.pdf)

**Turbochargers, superchargers, engine work, ECU (secondary sources)**
- Superchargers or turbochargers, and engine swaps, are listed as modifications that need LTA approval. — [Budget Direct Insurance](https://www.budgetdirect.com.sg/car-insurance/articles/car-modifications-impact); [Torque.com.sg](https://www.torque.com.sg/?p=62837)
- Any turbocharger or supercharger change needs prior LTA approval and evaluation before the work is done. Engine swaps are only allowed with the same engine type. Increasing engine capacity (for example boring out the cylinders) and nitrous injection are not allowed. — [AXL.sg, Car modification rules in Singapore](https://axl.sg/guides/car-modifications-singapore-rules); [Torque, what you can or cannot do](https://www.torque.com.sg/advice/modifying-cars-singapore-what-you-can-cannot-do/); [CarVault](https://www.carvault.com.sg/posts/car-modifications-in-singapore-will-modifying-your-car-void-your-warrantytle-here) (exact attribution among these three is unclear in the extract)
- **ECU remapping isn't mentioned on LTA's modification page.** The general rule against compromising emission and noise standards covers any remap tied to a turbo, exhaust or engine change. — [AXL.sg](https://axl.sg/guides/car-modifications-singapore-rules)
- Other items reported as needing approval: sunroofs, transmission changes, hood or canopy additions. Bull bars are banned because they can worsen pedestrian injuries. — [Torque.com.sg](https://www.torque.com.sg/?p=62837)

**Suspension, wheels, aero, lights (secondary)**
- A Motorist.sg Q&A lists suspension systems, spoilers, bumpers, side skirts, sports rims (cars only) and fog lamps as needing no approval. — [Motorist.sg, What car mods can I do without LTA approval?](https://www.motorist.sg/ask-motorists/3846/what-car-mods-can-i-do-without-lta-approval)
- AXL adds conditions. Bumpers, spoilers and side skirts must be maker-approved. Springs, shocks, anti-roll bars and strut bars come with conditions. Fog lamps need an approved marking. — [AXL.sg](https://axl.sg/guides/car-modifications-singapore-rules)
- Fog lamps also appear on SingSaver's no-approval list. — [SingSaver](https://www.singsaver.com.sg/blog/will-car-modifications-affect-my-car-insurance-premiums)
- Audio and multimedia equipment is allowed if permanently installed and without an independent power source. — [Budget Direct](https://www.budgetdirect.com.sg/car-insurance/articles/car-modifications-impact)

**Window tint (secondary, mostly consistent)**
- Windscreen and front side windows need at least **70%** visible light transmission, and rear windows at least **25%**. Film must be non-reflective. VLT is measured through glass and film together, and it is checked at periodic inspection. — [Solartint.sg](https://solartint.sg/); [Project Modz](https://www.projectmodz.sg/car-window-tint-singapore-best-solar-film-for-heat-rejection-uv-protection-lta-compliance/); [Infratint (2026 guide)](https://www.infratint.com/blog/lta-window-tint-regulations-singapore)
- Outlier: Zenith Car Detailing gives front side windows 25% and no minimum for rear windows. That contradicts the others. — [Zenith Car Detailing](https://zenithcardetailing.com/blog/what-tint-is-lta-approved-in-singapore/)
- Heat-reducing films get no exemption from the limits. — [CoverConcierge, 2026](https://coverconcierge.co/guides/window-tint-singapore/)
- Sources disagree on whether tinting needs LTA approval: Budget Direct says it is allowed within the limits, Torque lists it as approval-needed. — [Budget Direct](https://www.budgetdirect.com.sg/blog/car-insurance/car-modifications-impact); [Torque](https://www.torque.com.sg/?p=62837)

### Inferences
- A Singapore configurator offering HKS and Tomei parts should mark **exhausts, turbo kits and engine-capacity changes** as "LTA approval needed / subject to approval". It should probably not offer decat or test-pipe options, nor show capacity-increase kits as road-legal.
- Because LTA approval is per make and model and needs maker certification plus lab test reports, an exhaust that is legal in Japan isn't automatically legal in Singapore. Approval needs paperwork from HKS/Garage R or from Tomei. Foreign certification doesn't automatically carry over (one secondary guide says this about Euro 5 motorcycle exhausts: [Lazada auto guide](https://auto.lazada.sg/how-to-choose-an-lta-compliant-aftermarket-motorcycle-exhaust-20260807/)).
- With workshop fines up to S$40,000 since Feb 2026 and LTA actively prosecuting workshops, the site's copy should avoid implying that unapproved parts are road-legal. A line such as "fitted subject to LTA approval" or "for track use" is the safer default for approval-needed categories.

## 3. Emissions: in-use standards, the VES, and how modifications affect emission checks

### Takeaway
NEA tightened in-use petrol standards from **1 April 2018**. Before that only carbon monoxide was limited; from that date hydrocarbon limits apply too. All vehicles go through mandatory periodic inspection, which is where tailpipe emissions (and tint) are checked. LTA's exhaust-approval route explicitly needs emission test reports, and removing the catalytic converter is banned. I couldn't verify the current numeric limits or VES details.

### Cited Findings
- NEA announced in March 2017 that it would tighten exhaust standards for in-use petrol vehicles from 1 April 2018. Previously only CO was limited; hydrocarbon limits were added for all in-use petrol vehicles and most motorcycles. — [NEA news release](https://www.nea.gov.sg/media/news/news/reducing-pollution-from-in-use-vehicles-to-achieve-better-air-quality)
- NEA uses tailpipe exhaust monitoring to catch polluting vehicles. — [NEA EPD Annual Report 2018 (PDF)](https://www.nea.gov.sg/docs/default-source/resource/publications/environmental-protection-division-annual-report/epd-report-2018-v4-(compressed).pdf)
- DieselNet lists an in-use petrol CO limit of 3.5% and says all vehicles are subject to mandatory periodic inspection. The figure is undated and may predate the 2018 change. — [DieselNet, Emission Standards: Singapore](https://dieselnet.com/standards/sg/)
- At registration, cars must meet NEA exhaust emission standards, with testing for CO, HC, NOx and particulate matter. This is import and registration context, not in-use. — [LTA car importation guidelines (PDF)](https://onemotoring.lta.gov.sg/content/dam/onemotoring/Buying/PDF/Importation_Guidelines/Car.pdf)
- The Vehicular Emissions Scheme (VES) is catalogued by the IEA as a Singapore policy. I couldn't read its details in this session. — [IEA policy database, Vehicle Emissions Scheme](https://www.iea.org/policies/18860-vehicule-emissions-scheme)
- Exhaust modifications need lab test reports showing compliance with the noise **and emission** standards, and catalytic converter and muffler removal is banned because it worsens emissions. — [LTA OneMotoring](https://onemotoring.lta.gov.sg/content/onemotoring/home/owning/modification.html) (search extract)

### Inferences
- For a modified Evo IX, the practical emission risks are a removed or hollowed cat, aggressive fuelling from ECU or turbo upgrades, and test pipes. Each could fail the periodic tailpipe check (CO and, since 2018, HC) as well as break the exhaust rules.
- The VES is understood to be a registration-time CO2 and pollutant rebate/surcharge scheme. If so, it wouldn't apply directly to aftermarket parts fitted to an already registered car. This is background understanding, **not verified this session**. Confirm it on LTA's VES page before relying on it.

### Gaps
- The current numeric in-use limits for petrol cars (CO %, HC ppm) and the noise limit in dB(A) for in-use cars weren't found. The Environmental Protection and Management (Vehicular Emissions) Regulations on Singapore Statutes Online hold them, but that host was blocked.
- VES bands and their current (2025–2026) form weren't researched in detail.
- No source says how often periodic inspection happens for a car of the Evo IX's age, or what an inspection checks for modified intakes or ECUs.

## 4. Is there an LTA list of approved aftermarket exhausts, and are HKS or Tomei exhausts on it?

### Takeaway
**I found no published LTA list of approved aftermarket car exhausts**, and no evidence either way that any HKS or Tomei exhaust is LTA-approved. Official sources describe a **per-vehicle approval process**: maker's certificate, recognised-lab noise and emission reports, then inspection. That is not a product whitelist.

### Cited Findings
- The approval route is per make and model: maker's certification that the system suits the vehicle, plus noise and emission test reports from an LTA- or NEA-recognised lab, submitted for evaluation before fitting. — [LTA OneMotoring](https://onemotoring.lta.gov.sg/content/onemotoring/home/owning/modification.html) (search extract)
- LTA allows only certified exhausts that are tested to international standards and compatible with the specific make and model. — [LTA newsroom, 2021](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/newsroom/2021/5/news-releases/workshop-owner-charged-for-illegal-exhaust-modifications.html)
- Motorcycle-focused retailer guides mention checking an "LTA approved list" and looking for an LTA-approved plate or stamp on the canister. These are secondary, motorcycle-specific, and I couldn't confirm that such a list exists for cars. — [Lazada auto guide](https://auto.lazada.sg/how-to-choose-an-lta-compliant-aftermarket-motorcycle-exhaust-20260807/); [Lazada, jelly-can exhaust guide](https://auto.lazada.sg/will-a-jelly-can-exhaust-pass-singapore-lta-vehicle-inspection-sg/)
- A Motorist.sg answer (motorcycle) describes the documents as test reports from LTA- or NEA-recognised labs for the specific model. — [Motorist.sg Q&A](https://www.motorist.sg/ask-motorists/1838/is-the-2020-j-h-exhaust-lta-street-legal-for-motorcycle-i)

### Inferences
- For the configurator, "LTA-approved HKS exhaust" should not be claimed unless Garage R (for HKS) or the workshop has the approval paperwork for that part on the Evo IX. Exhaust options are better worded as "subject to LTA approval".
- The exclusive HKS distributor, Garage R, is the most likely holder of HKS test reports and certificates for Singapore approvals. That is a reason to source HKS exhausts through its dealer network. Not confirmed.

### Gaps
- Whether LTA publishes any approved car-exhaust list (onemotoring.lta.gov.sg was blocked).
- Whether any HKS or Tomei Evo IX exhaust has ever been approved in Singapore.
- No dB(A) limit for cars was confirmed. The often-quoted "90 dB(A)" figure is **unverified**.
