## 1. Which cat-back exhausts exist for the Evo IX, and what are their specs (part number, piping, material, silencer, tips, position, noise certification, status)?

### Takeaway
Every Evo IX cat-back found from these brands is a **single round tip at the factory exit, under the right side of the rear bumper**. No twin, quad or centre exits exist for the CT9A from any of them. What changes between products is the tip: Ø90–124 mm; polished, burnt-blue titanium, raw titanium or Blitz's titanium-colour curl; straight, slash or angled cut. The JDM brands publish JASMA certification and dB figures:
- **Fujitsubo:** Legalis R, RM-01A, RM-01S and Super Ti, all current.
- **Kakimoto:** Regu.06 & R, Hyper Fullmega N1+ Rev. and Kakimoto.R, all current. The GT1.0Z Racing is competition-only at 103 dB.
- **Blitz:** both Evo IX Nür-Spec systems (VSR 62119V and the competition-only Nür-Spec R MM3040) are **discontinued**.

Fujitsubo has **no Authorize**, Kakimoto **no Class KR**, Blitz **no VS or F-Ti**, and Invidia **no Q300, Gemini or R400** for the Evo IX. Invidia offers only the N1, with a polished or titanium tip. The two US brands publish no noise data:
- **Invidia:** the N1, with a polished or titanium tip.
- **ETS:** 3.0"/3.5" titanium and stainless systems with a 4" tip angled at 15°, in JDM and USDM bumper versions.

## 5. Singapore legality: LTA approval, noise, the catalytic-converter ban, and which items could never be legal on a Singapore road car

### Takeaway
LTA requires **prior approval for any exhaust change**. The application needs the maker's certificate that the system suits the make and model, plus noise and emission test reports from an LTA- or NEA-recognised lab. **Removing catalytic converters or mufflers is not allowed.** Penalties rose from 27 Feb 2026.

So every **cat-delete or competition-only item** here could never be legal on a Singapore road car:
- Kakimoto catalyst silencer MCS305 and catalyst straight MSP305
- Tomei Ti cat straight pipe 431003
- ETS's "downpipe-back" race exhaust
- Kakimoto GT1.0Z Racing (103 dB)
- Blitz Nür-Spec R
- Tomei Expreme Ti ("off-road use only")

The JASMA-certified Japanese mufflers and the cat-retaining Japanese front pipes are the only items with any certification to start an application from. Even those are "subject to LTA approval", not "LTA-approved". No LTA list of approved car exhausts was found (earlier notes).

### Cited Findings
- **Approval route:** you must get LTA approval before replacing or changing the exhaust system, submitting the maker's certification for your make and model plus noise and emission test reports from LTA- or NEA-recognised labs. — [LTA OneMotoring, Vehicle modifications](https://onemotoring.lta.gov.sg/content/onemotoring/home/owning/modification.html) (search extract, via `singapore_market_and_lta.md`)
- **Cat and muffler ban:** "You are not allowed to remove catalytic converters and mufflers, as it will adversely affect the exhaust emission characteristics of the vehicle." — [LTA OneMotoring](https://onemotoring.lta.gov.sg/content/onemotoring/home/owning/modification.html) (search extract, via earlier notes)
- **Certified systems only:** only certified exhaust systems "which have undergone stringent testing in compliance with international standards and are compatible with that particular make and model" are allowed. — [LTA newsroom, May 2021](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/newsroom/2021/5/news-releases/workshop-owner-charged-for-illegal-exhaust-modifications.html)
- **Penalties:** raised from 27 Feb 2026 to up to S$20,000 and/or 2 years' jail for individuals, and up to S$40,000 for workshops. Repeat offenders face double. — [The Independent SG](https://theindependent.sg/lta-warns-against-illegal-vehicle-mods-as-workshop-fined-and-penalties-raised/) (via earlier notes). A workshop was fined S$6,000 in 2026 for illegal exhaust modifications, and 26 workshops have been charged since 2021. — [Mothership, Apr 2026](https://mothership.sg/2026/04/workshop-fined-illegal-modification-exhaust-singapore/) (via earlier notes)
- **Noise limit:** no Singapore dB(A) limit for in-use cars was confirmed. The often-quoted "90 dB(A)" is unverified. — `singapore_market_and_lta.md`
- **Japanese limits for comparison:** 96 dB(A) proximity (safety standard), 95 dB(A) (JASMA) for pre-2010 front-engined cars. — [Fujitsubo regulations page](https://www.fujitsubo.co.jp/about-exhaust-systems/regulation/)
- **Maker legal markings:**
  - Kakimoto marks GT1.0Z Racing, the catalyst silencer and the catalyst straight as "競技専用品 (一般公道使用不可)", competition only, not usable on public roads, sold only with Kakimoto's competition-use purchase application form. — [Kakimoto rid=769](https://www.kakimotoracing.co.jp/products/detail_product.cgi?rid=769); [rid=787](https://www.kakimotoracing.co.jp/products/detail_product.cgi?rid=787); [rid=791](https://www.kakimotoracing.co.jp/products/detail_product.cgi?rid=791)
  - Blitz lists the Nür-Spec R as 競技専用品. — [Blitz Nür-Spec R application list](https://partsnavi.blitz.co.jp/products/search/search_products/list.php?maker_id=5&category_id=7&ids=36_76&car_name=%E3%83%A9%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B5%E3%83%BC%E3%82%A8%E3%83%9C%E3%83%AA%E3%83%A5%E3%83%BC%E3%82%B7%E3%83%A7%E3%83%B3IX+%28LANCER+EVO+IX%29&model_year=&car_model=)
  - Fujitsubo, Kakimoto (Regu.06 & R, N1+ Rev., Kakimoto.R) and Blitz VSR carry JASMA certificate numbers. Fujitsubo, Kakimoto and Blitz front pipes are marked 保安基準適合 (meets Japan's safety standards). — Table 1 and Table 2 sources

### Inferences
- **Configurator wording:** list exhausts and front pipes as "subject to LTA approval", never "LTA-approved", unless the workshop holds approval paperwork for that part on an Evo IX. Do not offer cat-delete parts at all.
- **Front pipes:** they are part of the exhaust system, so they also need approval (inferred from the LTA wording, as in the earlier notes). Their advantage is that they keep the factory cat.
- **Loudest JASMA options:** Fujitsubo Super Ti (92 dB proximity, 79 dB idle) and Kakimoto (93 dB proximity) sit near the Japanese 95 dB(A) JASMA ceiling. With Singapore's limit unknown, the quieter Fujitsubo RM-01A or Legalis R (87–88 dB) may pass a static test more easily. This is an inference, not a tested result.

### Gaps
- **Singapore noise rules:** the numeric in-use noise limit and the test method are unconfirmed. The OneMotoring and Singapore Statutes Online pages were not reachable in this or the earlier session.
- **Approval record:** no evidence was found either way that any of these specific exhausts has been approved by LTA on an Evo IX.
- **Foreign certification:** whether LTA accepts JASMA certificates as the "maker's certification" or as noise test evidence is unknown.
- **High-flow cats:** whether a high-flow catalytic converter (ETS GESI, HKS Metal Catalyzer) counts as "removing" the factory converter in LTA's eyes is unclear. Treat it as needing approval and emission testing.
