# Evo IX parts across more brands: Singapore sourcing channels and LTA rules by modification type

Builds on `research_notes/Tomei and HKS Evo IX parts/singapore_market_and_lta.md`, which covers HKS (Garage R, exclusive SG/MY distributor), Tomei (no SG distributor; Blackhawk Japan and RZ Crew Garage), ENDLESS, the LTA three-tier framework, the exhaust approval route, tint, cat removal, and the Feb 2026 penalties. Those points are not repeated here except where this round adds to or corrects them.

**Method note for the report writer (researched 26 Nov 2026):**
- Direct page fetches failed for every Singapore host tried: `onemotoring.lta.gov.sg`, `www.lta.gov.sg`, `sso.agc.gov.sg` (Singapore Statutes Online), `www.onemotoring.com.sg`, `www.mnd.gov.sg`, `axl.sg`, `sgpdriver.com` and `fatcarexport.com`. They would have to be allowed in the session's network settings.
- The search tool also refuses `straitstimes.com`, `channelnewsasia.com`, `todayonline.com` and `sgcarmart.com`.
- **Every finding below therefore comes from search-engine extracts**, not full page reads. Where an extract comes from an official LTA page it is marked "(LTA, search extract)". Secondary sources are labelled as such.
- There are about 80 brands. I searched them in groups by type, so **some brands were never searched by name**. They are marked "not searched by name" in the table. That means only that no Singapore channel turned up in the group searches, not that a search for that brand came back empty.

## 1. Brand → Singapore channel (official distributor, regional distributor, or retail/grey import)

### Takeaway
Only a few of the listed brands have an identifiable official Singapore distributor:
- **YHI Corporation (Singapore):** Yokohama/Advan tyres, Enkei, OZ Racing
- **Stamford Tyres:** Rays (Volk Racing, Gram Lights), with WedsSport on show at its outlet and Toyo in its brand portfolio
- **Bridgestone Tyre Sales Singapore:** Bridgestone
- **Turbo One:** most likely Garrett (replacement turbos)
- **Fong Kim Exhaust System:** BC Racing
- **Braking Point:** AP Racing (from the earlier notes)

A few brands are served regionally from Malaysia or Thailand: BBS and Recaro (Autovogue Performance, Malaysia), KW (KW Automotive Sdn Bhd, Malaysia) and Öhlins (Öhlins Asia, Thailand). Almost every other brand on the list (most Japanese and US tuning brands, engine internals, fuel parts, clutches, aero, seats, steering wheels, gauges and harnesses) reaches Singapore only through **Japan- or US-sourcing retailers that price in SGD**, chiefly **Blackhawk Japan (blackhawkjapan.sg)** and **RZ Crew Garage (rzcrewgarage.com)**, or through marketplaces (Carousell, Desertcart, Ubuy, eBay). In practice these are grey imports.

### Inferences
- The site can honestly say "sourced through the official Singapore distributor" only for **Yokohama/Advan tyres, Enkei, OZ (YHI); Rays/Volk/Gram Lights (Stamford Tyres); Bridgestone; BC Racing (Fong Kim); AP Racing (Braking Point)**, and HKS via Garage R from the earlier notes. Everything else is safer worded as "imported to order".
- The distributor matters for LTA paperwork. Exhaust and turbo approvals need the **manufacturer's certification for the make and model plus recognised-lab test reports** (see section 2). For grey-import brands (Fujitsubo, Kakimoto, Invidia, Blitz, Forced Performance, Kinugawa) the workshop would have to get those documents from the maker itself, which is a real barrier in practice.
- Grey channels carry counterfeit risk (Carousell "replica" Nardi wheels; Endless and Rays fakes are widely discussed). Retailers such as Blackhawk Japan source in Japan but give no SG brand warranty. Under BBS Japan's stated policy, only official-dealer wheels get local warranty and support.
- That Garage R, the official HKS distributor, was reportedly itself fined in March 2026 for illegal exhaust work suggests "official distributor" doesn't guarantee an LTA-compliant install. The configurator's legal flags should rest on the part type, not the brand.

### Gaps
- About 35 brands were **not searched by name** (marked above), mostly US engine internals, fuel parts, cooling, mounts and some Japanese aero, chassis and LSD brands. Their SG channel is assumed to be grey import, which is unverified.
- Advan Racing **wheels** (Yokohama Wheel): no SG distributor found. YHI's role for wheels is unknown.
- Whether Stamford Tyres still holds the Rays agreement in 2026 (the CarBuyer article is undated; the 2021 article confirms Rays stock).
- Garrett's **performance** distributor for Singapore (separate from the replacement network) wasn't visible in the locator extract.
- Official SG distributors for Tein, Cusco, Bride, Recaro, Defi, MOMO, Work, SSR, Blitz, Exedy and Öhlins (car) weren't found. Ask the brands directly.
- No Evo IX (CT9A) SGD prices were collected for this brand set, apart from the Blitz exhaust in the earlier notes.

## 2. LTA rules by modification type, the approval and inspection process, and the 2026 penalty changes

### Takeaway
LTA sorts modifications into **no approval / approval needed / not allowed**.
- **No approval, but conditions apply:** wheels and tyres (manufacturer's size, no protrusion beyond the body, same rolling radius), suspension (the body must never touch the road, no cutting of bodywork), spoilers and body kits (no sharp edges, within the car's width and length), and seats (original anchorages).
- **Prior LTA approval needed:** exhausts, turbo/supercharger changes, engine swaps (same engine type only) and any powertrain change (a CVP application through VITAS, S$66). By one Motorist Q&A, a non-original bonnet material such as carbon also needs approval.
- **Not allowed:** removing the catalytic converter or muffler, increasing engine capacity, nitrous, chassis changes, tow hooks, crash bars, air horns, aftermarket headlamps and HID kits, tinted lamps, and decorative or neon lighting.

Since **27 Feb 2026**, illegal modification carries up to **S$20,000 and/or 2 years' jail** for individuals and up to **S$40,000 (S$80,000 for repeat offenders) for workshops and other non-individuals**. LTA has said it plans tighter powertrain-modification rules (VAC 34/2025).

### Inferences
- **The Evo IX's age matters.** The CT9A was built 2005–07, so any Singapore Evo IX is well over 10 years old and goes through **annual periodic inspection**. Every year the inspector sees wheel protrusion, tint, lamps, exhaust and tow hooks. EIR adds more inspections if engine or exhaust offences repeat.
- **Steering wheels:** The Evo IX has a driver's airbag. A MOMO, Nardi or Works Bell hub setup removes it, which the "consult the manufacturer" condition effectively rules out (no carmaker endorses removing an airbag). The configurator should flag aftermarket steering wheels as "not road-legal / track only".
- **Front-mount intercoolers, intakes, BOVs, fuel parts, ECU:** no official rule. The safest copy is "fitted as part of an LTA-approved engine package" or "subject to LTA approval". Don't imply that bolt-on power parts need no approval.
- **Big wings (Voltex, APR GTC) and canards:** the conditions are "no sharp edges" and "within the car's width and length". A large GT wing whose end plates or overhang go past the body, or sharp-edged canards, would fail. Smaller, rounded parts that sit within the body outline are the road-compatible versions.
- **Roll cages and harnesses:** there is no explicit rule, and harnesses aren't approved belts. Mark them "track use", or "keep the factory belts fitted alongside", pending LTA or inspection-centre confirmation.

### Gaps
- No **minimum ground-clearance** figure, **wheel-offset** limit or **tyre load/speed-index** rule was found. The Road Traffic (Motor Vehicles, Construction and Use) Rules on Singapore Statutes Online would hold any such rule; that host was blocked.
- The **current in-use exhaust noise limit** for cars (103 dB(A) in the 2001 text versus 99 dB(A) on a blog; UN-standard adoption from 2023) is unverified.
- Car road tax bands above 1,000 cc and the age surcharge weren't confirmed from official text (calculator sites conflict).
- Not addressed by any source found: brakes, clutch and LSD, fuel system, intercooler, gauges, roll cage (saloon) and harnesses.
- The exact enacted text and in-force date of LTA's planned powertrain-rule tightening (VAC 34/2025) weren't found.
- Approval fees and processing times for exhaust and turbo approvals weren't found (only the S$66 CVP fee).

## 3. Which items on such a list are never road-legal, and which need prior LTA approval

### Takeaway
For an Evo IX parts list:
- **Never road-legal:** stroker or capacity-increase kits, decat or test pipes and muffler deletes, nitrous, protruding tow hooks, aftermarket HID kits and headlamps or tinted lamps, slick or off-road tyres, wheels or tyres sticking out past the arches, chassis cutting or welding, decorative or neon lighting, and in practice airbag-delete steering wheels.
- **Prior approval needed:** exhausts (including downpipes and manifolds with a cat retained), turbo upgrades and any powertrain change (CVP), engine swaps (same type only) and, per Motorist, a non-original-material (carbon) bonnet.
- **No approval, with conditions:** wheels, tyres, coilovers and arms, wings and aero within the body outline, seats on original anchorages, tint within limits and approved fog lamps.
- **Unclear:** intercoolers, intakes, BOVs, ECU, fuel parts, brakes, clutch and LSD, gauges, roll cages and harnesses.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Approval items need **manufacturer documents for the make and model plus recognised-lab test reports**. For a 2005–07 car and grey-import brands, many exhausts and turbo kits may be impractical to approve even though they are not banned outright. The configurator could show these as "LTA approval required — RZER to advise".
- Given the 2026 workshop penalties (up to S$40,000, S$80,000 for repeat offenders) and LTA prosecuting workshops, the configurator should not show banned items (decat, stroker, nitrous, tow hooks, airbag-delete wheels) as road options. If they are listed at all, it should be as "track / off-road use only" with a clear note.

### Gaps
- No official LTA text was read in full (host blocked). The approval, condition and ban statuses above rest on official search extracts plus secondary sources, and should be checked on OneMotoring and with VICOM, STA or JIC before the site states them.
- Whether semi-slick R-compound tyres (Advan A052, Toyo R888R, Bridgestone RE71RS-class tyres are street-legal elsewhere) count as "slicks" under LTA's tyre rule is unknown.
- Whether same-capacity engine internals, cams and fuel parts need a CVP or approval in practice is unknown (VAC 34/2025 text wasn't read in full).
