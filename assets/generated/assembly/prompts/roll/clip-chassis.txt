Seen exactly from the front, the car builds itself around the single wheel like a transforming machine, with precise mechanical movements, everything at its real-life size and proportions: the wheel never turns or tilts and keeps its tread facing the camera; a drive shaft extends sideways out of its hub and the wheel slides sideways to the right into its place as the car's front wheel, while an identical front wheel slides out to the left on the other end of the axle; two rear wheels roll in straight out of the darkness behind them; the steel chassis frame rails, the subframes and the red coilovers unfold and extend between the wheels; then the engine, turbo, radiator and intercooler rise into place at the front, and the seats and steering wheel unfold behind, until the bare rolling chassis of the last frame stands in the pool of light. Every wheel always faces the camera the same way as in the last frame, and no part is ever bigger than in the last frame. Locked-off camera: the camera does not move, turn or zoom at all, and the dark workshop, the ceiling, the red neon and the floor stay perfectly still; only the car's parts move. Photorealistic, cinematic, one continuous shot. No people, no robots, no text, no logos, no scene cut.
